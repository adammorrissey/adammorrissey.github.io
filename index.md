## AI & Data Perspectives

The conversation around AI in insurance and financial services has matured. The question is no longer *if* — it's *how much value are you actually capturing, and what's blocking the rest?*

<div class="perspective-card">
<h3>The Data Readiness Gap Is the Real Problem</h3>
<p>McKinsey research consistently shows that AI projects stall not because the model underperforms, but because the underlying data isn't clean, governed, or structured for machine consumption. The unsexy work — data quality, ETL, taxonomy alignment, lineage documentation — is the actual competitive moat. Organizations that invest in foundations before models will capture more value, faster.</p>
</div>

<div class="perspective-card">
<h3>Adoption Is the Hard Part, Not the Model</h3>
<p>Fortune 500 companies are spending billions on AI capabilities that frontline employees never meaningfully use. The value capture problem is a change management and workflow integration challenge as much as a technology challenge. A dashboard no one trusts, a tool no one opens — that's a failed AI investment regardless of the model's benchmark scores.</p>
</div>

<div class="perspective-card">
<h3>Insurance Is an AI-Native Opportunity in Disguise</h3>
<p>Insurance is fundamentally a data business — pricing risk, detecting fraud, predicting churn, modeling catastrophe exposure. The industry that historically lagged on analytics modernization now has a rare chance to leapfrog. Companies that move decisively on claims automation, telematics-driven pricing, and AI-assisted underwriting in the next 24 months will set cost structures and pricing floors that late movers simply cannot match.</p>
</div>

<div class="perspective-card">
<h3>The WSJ-to-Workflow Problem</h3>
<p>Every week brings another headline about AI transforming an industry. The hard question — the one I find most interesting — is the gap between what's in the Wall Street Journal and what's actually deployed in production inside a mid-size insurer or regional carrier. Closing that gap requires people who understand both the technology and the business well enough to translate between them. That's the role I'm building toward.</p>
</div>
